Ambulance vs. Driving Yourself

In severe conditions (chest pain, stroke, unconsciousness, severe trauma), always wait for an ambulance (108/112).

  • Paramedics start life-saving cardiac/oxygen care in transit.
  • Ambulances bypass traffic delays and signal hospitals ahead.

Call the Hospital Ahead

If you are transporting someone yourself, have a second person call the nearest emergency room while on the way:

  • "Is an active trauma/ICU team and bed available right now?"
  • For suspected stroke/heart attack: "Is a CT scan / Cath Lab ready?"

What to Bring to the Hospital

Have a bystander quickly grab these without delaying immediate transport:

  • Current Medications / Prescriptions: Helps doctors prevent lethal drug interactions.
  • Govt ID & Insurance Card: For quick casualty admission.

What Should I Do Next?

1

Check Scene Safety

Ensure you and the person are away from traffic, electrical hazards, fire, or falling objects before approaching.

2

Check Responsiveness

Gently tap their shoulders and ask loudly: "Are you okay?" Look if their chest is rising and falling normally.

3

Call 112 & Follow Instructions

Put your phone on speaker mode. State your precise location clearly and follow the dispatcher's exact step-by-step orders.

      Preparedness & Protocol

      How to Communicate in an Emergency

      Staying calm and delivering structured information saves crucial minutes for paramedics and emergency ambulances.

      Dispatcher Checklist

      • Exact Address & Landmark: Give building names, floor numbers, or prominent crossroads first.
      • Consciousness & Breathing: State whether the patient is awake and if they are breathing regularly.
      • Stay on the line: Never hang up until the operator tells you to. They will guide you continuously.

      What NOT to Do

      • Do not offer fluids or food: If the person loses consciousness or needs surgery, this can cause life-threatening choking (aspiration).
      • Do not crowd the patient: Allow maximum airflow and have one person wait outside to guide the ambulance crew.
      • Never put items in the mouth: Do not place spoons, keys, or fingers in a seizing person's mouth.

      About the PulseLab Project

      PulseLab is a student-led educational initiative exploring how principles of biomedical engineering, human-factor interaction design, and healthcare technologies can improve community emergency response times.

      During medical emergencies, panic and disinformation create harmful delays. PulseLab acts as an accessible, rapid cognitive aid to bridge the critical interval between symptom onset and arrival at an emergency healthcare facility.

      Rapid Accessibility

      Zero barriers: No account required, lightweight performance, mobile responsive, and free from personal data storage.

      Public Awareness

      Promoting public understanding of acute red-flag indicators such as stroke (FAST) and silent cardiac presentations.

      Prompt Action

      Direct telecommunication integration with India's emergency response support system (ERSS 112).

      Engineering Ethics

      Clear boundaries between supportive health education tools and licensed diagnostic medical devices.

      Academic & Emergency Safety Disclaimer

      PulseLab is an educational project, not a medical service. It does not diagnose, treat, or replace professional medical advice. In an emergency, call 112 immediately. Follow instructions from emergency responders. PulseLab does not store, collect, or transmit any user health data.
